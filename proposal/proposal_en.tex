\begin{abstract}
\noindent This project takes the web novel \textit{Xiaobing Chuanqi} as its world-building
blueprint and will be developed with the Unity engine and Blender assets, producing a playable
game demo themed on interstellar warfare and driven by a faction's rise from the bottom.
The central design idea is to let the three playable factions---the Federation, the Silver Eagle
Empire and the Family---share one underlying layer of business logic (the relation network, the
combat system and the star map), differing only in starting conditions and growth resources,
so that content reuse is maximised within a limited schedule. This document states the motivation,
the comparison with existing works, the system design and the implementation plan, and reports the
completed setting analysis, system design, clickable prototype and compilable C\# engineering skeleton.
\end{abstract}

\section{Topic and Goals}

We choose the interstellar-war setting for two reasons: it borrows the positive feedback of
production and scale expansion from \textit{Dyson Sphere Program}, and it reuses the complete
setting already provided by \textit{Xiaobing Chuanqi}, which lowers the cost of world building.
The novel supplies four forms of government, a six-tier twenty-one-rank military hierarchy,
an A--J ship rating table, a bipolar order of two great powers plus the fragmented Wuluan system,
and a four-stage main storyline that runs from recruit to the unification of the universe.
These settings convert directly into numeric baselines and a narrative skeleton.

We plan to deliver an operable game demo supporting at least one fully playable faction flow.
The player grows either through military merit (Federation, Silver Eagle) or through resource
expansion (Family), and in the late game enters the same layer of resource and political contention.
AI is applied inside the game: an AI adjutant modelled on Xingling, the personified central computer
of the novel, provides tactical advice and intelligence analysis. The project does not aim at
algorithmic novelty; it is evaluated on the completeness and playability of the demo itself.

and letting the three factions reuse the common modules to lower implementation cost.

\section{System Design}
The main storyline is currently designed as three branches: the Federation line, the Silver Eagle
line and the Family line.

\subsection{Differences and Balance Among the Three Factions}
The Federation line follows Tang Long. In the early game high command handles resource scheduling
while the player concentrates on combat, and must cope with merit being claimed by others and with
factional infighting. The Silver Eagle line is similar at the start, but its subordinates are
absolutely loyal and the conflict comes from the succession struggle among princes and the
alignment of nobles. The Family line builds resources and industry from nothing: high freedom,
simple relations.

The three lines are comparable in the early game: the combat lines are spared logistics, and the
Family line is spared politics. Growth paths are isomorphic, and once the player is promoted into
high command all three converge on the same resource and political layer.

\subsection{Story Progression and Resource Scheduling}
The main line is organised around the four-stage conflict of the novel: the recruit period, the
founding of the Wuluan state, the Anti-Tang alliance, and the unification of the universe.
Two or three pivotal choices change the ending; they are triggered jointly by the relation network,
military merit and resource stock. Resources share one underlying set (funds, rare metals, ships,
population, energy) and differ only in when each becomes visible to the player.

\noindent\textbf{Division of labour}: Member A, Gao Zihao, owns the real combat simulation and
touches only \path{com.starpawn.combat} and \path{content/ships}. Member B, Zhang Rongbo, owns the
Unity playable shell, strategic rules, the relation network, mission narrative, the AI adjutant and
final assembly, and touches the remaining packages and \path{Assets/Game}. The two meet only at
\path{BattleStartSpec} and \path{SettleBattle}; \path{CombatContracts} is frozen for now.
Both work in parallel on the same battle during week one and join at the battle-result interface
on day five.

\textbf{Resource estimate}: two people, about twelve weeks; the stack is Unity for runtime and
Blender for assets (exported as glTF/FBX). The main needs are development machines and art assets;
no extra computing power or data purchase is required.

\textbf{Results so far}: we have completed a full reading of \textit{Xiaobing Chuanqi} and three
setting-analysis documents, and produced a concept poster and the system architecture design, plus a
clickable single-file prototype with five views---star map, relation network, combat, resources and
narrative---whose combat page is a real-time 3D battle HUD. On the engineering side, twelve Unity
packages and twenty-one assemblies form a skeleton that separates contracts from fake implementations,
with cross-module references forbidden at compile time, and all twenty-one assemblies do compile.
Fourteen scenario blocks of contract tests cover duplicate settlement, rollback on mid-way failure,
inheritance of battle damage, snapshot isolation and content validation. Ships, factions, star map,
missions, story and the mission log are all stored as JSON with reference validation, so rewards and
hull attributes change by editing configuration. Code repository:
\url{https://github.com/gaozihao1216/NLP-project}.

The topic, the system design decisions and the final content were determined and checked by the
group members.
